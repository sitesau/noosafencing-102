How coast and hinterland change the brief

Noosa is not one fencing environment. Beachfront and exposed strips are described as places where salt air and wind influence material choice, while hinterland runs can raise different access, acreage and licensing questions. That does not make one material universally better; it means the brief should match the conditions.

  • Colorbond and aluminium slat are presented as options suited to coastal architecture and exposed conditions.
  • Timber is described for established hinterland streets, acreage and residential blocks.
  • Pool fencing is treated separately because it is compliance-led, not only a visual boundary.
  • Rural fencing and gates add length, terrain, gate hardware and removal questions.

A sheltered side boundary, a beachside pool area and a hinterland paddock line can all sit inside the same broad region while needing different scopes.

Shared boundaries and timing to know

Where the fence divides two properties, the legal pathway can shape the quote before materials are final. The dividing fences framework named in the guidance is the Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011, which deals with adjoining owners considering a sufficient dividing fence. An owner who wants more than sufficient may need to cover the difference, so write down height, finish and any upgrade preference before comparing prices.

If neighbour discussion does not settle contribution, the formal step described is a notice to contribute, with one month allowed for agreement and a possible QCAT application within two months of the notice. Height also needs context: ground level, retaining walls and corner-block sight lines can affect approval. Pool barriers have separate safety requirements and an inspection pathway confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector.

What a comparable written quote should show

A low-looking number is hard to compare when removal, disposal, gate hardware or difficult digging are left vague. Ask each contractor to price the same written scope, not a loose metre rate.

  • Measured run and height.
  • Material, posts and gate details.
  • Old fence removal and disposal.
  • Access assumptions and excluded work.
  • Coastal wind and salt response, where relevant.
  • Licence scope for hinterland or acreage work, where relevant.
  • Inspection pathway for pool barriers, where relevant.

A planning calculator can help shape a first budget range, but it is described as a conversation starter rather than a site measure or fixed offer.

Who this applies to

These questions suit owners comparing local fencing options before requesting a quote, especially where the job involves an exposed coastal boundary, a hinterland property, a shared dividing fence, a pool barrier, a gate or an old fence that must come out first. It is less useful for someone who already has a final measured scope, neighbour agreement, pool inspection advice and comparable written quotes.

At that later stage the decision narrows to contractor availability, inclusions and terms. Before then, the work is to make the site conditions legible: line, height, purpose, access, existing fence, compliance question and written assumptions.

  1. Describe the line. Send the suburb, approximate run, height, old fence details and access constraints before asking for a price.
  2. Name the purpose. State whether the fence is for privacy, a pool barrier, a front screen, a rural boundary or a driveway gate.
  3. Check shared boundaries. For dividing fences, record the proposed sufficient standard and whether any owner wants more than sufficient.
  4. Request a written scope. Ask for measured run, material, posts, gates, removal, disposal, exclusions and access assumptions.
  5. Treat calculators as prompts. Use a budget range to prepare better questions, not as a substitute for a site-specific quote.

Common questions

What if a neighbour will not agree to contribute? The guidance points to the dividing fence process rather than an informal standoff. A notice to contribute is described as the formal step, allowing one month to reach agreement, with a possible QCAT application within two months of the notice.

Is a pool fence quoted like a normal boundary fence? No. Pool fencing is treated as compliance-led work with separate safety requirements and an inspection pathway confirmed by a licensed pool safety inspector.

Is the cheapest metre rate the best comparison? Not by itself. A low number may not be comparable if removal, disposal, gate hardware, difficult digging or access limits are vague. Compare the written scope line by line.
